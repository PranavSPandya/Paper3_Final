# H4 results report

## Direct H4: perceived thermal comfort (exploratory correction)
Hypothesis: noise amplifies thermal discomfort. The traffic component remains untested: the current YAMNet vehicle metrics have provisional category definitions/cutoffs and are not a validated traffic exposure.
Scoring verified against ExpData/scripts/05_questionnaire_build.py COMFORT_MAP_5: 1 extremely uncomfortable, 2 very uncomfortable, 3 non comfortable, 4 slightly non comfortable, 5 comfortable. Higher scores mean greater comfort.
44 distinct ratings from 11 participants, one per outdoor phase. Ratings copied into 10-second rows are counted once. 37/44 ratings are 4 or 5; lower categories are sparse.
Exposure means use the same jointly observed PET/UTCI/dBA windows per bout: 49-49 windows; 0 bouts have incomplete exposure coverage. They represent the selected eight-minute sensor window, not necessarily the whole phase recalled by the participant.
Design VIF (heat, noise, heat x noise, phase): PET 5.32, UTCI 4.97; values >= 5 are review flags, not exclusions.
Two specified representations, PET and UTCI, are analysed and reported together; neither is selected using its p-value. No PM alternatives or extra radiation/light terms are introduced into these small ordinal models.
Formula: ordered thermal comfort ~ Heat * Noise + PhaseID + (1 | ParticipantID). Cumulative logit / proportional odds, ordinal::clmm, 10 adaptive quadrature points; 20-point quadrature and leave-one-participant-out checks are diagnostics.
Heat = (bout-average PET or UTCI minus the across-bout mean) / 5 degrees C. Noise = (bout-average dBA minus the across-bout mean) / 5 dBA. Arithmetic mean dBA is used; it is not labelled LAeq. No bout-centering removes between-bout thermal variation in these direct models.
The interaction is the change in the heat log-odds slope per 5 dBA. A negative interaction means increasing noise makes the heat association with comfort more negative, consistent in direction with H4. OR_multiplier = exp(interaction Beta), a ratio of odds ratios; the intervals are approximate Wald intervals.
Participant random intercepts account for repeated ratings under the specified model; exposure coefficients pool within- and between-participant information and must not be called purely within-person or causal effects. Random effects are assumed independent of predictors. Phase is retained as an adjustment.
The planned test family is the two heat x noise interactions. Each likelihood-ratio statistic compares interaction versus identical main-effects model. 999 null parametric bootstrap replicates per representation simulate participant random intercepts and ordinal responses; seed 20261013. Failed/invalid replicates count against rejection when forming the reported upper p bound. If more than 5% fail, p/q are withheld. BH correction spans both tests, counting any unreportable test as a non-rejection.
Model-dependent bootstrap calibration does not establish proportional odds, random-effect exogeneity or representativeness. Those assumptions and the sparse 11-participant design prevent confirmatory claims. This revision was made after secondary results were seen, so the direct findings remain exploratory.

## Columns of the two results tables (plain language)
- equation: which heat measure the model uses (AIR_temp + AIR_RH + WIND_AWS, HUMIDEX, PET or UTCI). Each model includes sound, distinct radiation/light candidates, phase/exertion and one named PM representation.
- outcome: the ordered thermal-comfort rating for direct models, or a body/eye measure for secondary models.
- analysis_role / independent_unit / p_method: identify the analysis, observation unit and inference used; do not pool significance counts across them.
- term: the model input. 'ucm_X' = that exposure; 'ucm_X:SND_dBA' = heat x noise (does noise change the heat effect?); 'Phase: X vs mean of phases' = how phase X differs from the average phase.
- term_type: 'thermal exposure' = heat term or heat x noise term (what H4 tests); 'covariate' = other exposure held constant; 'phase (study design)' = route/mode condition; 'intercept' = baseline level.
- Beta: for direct ordinal interactions, change in heat log-odds slope per 5 dBA (heat per 5 degrees C). For secondary rows, outcome-unit change per one pooled SD of within-bout exposure deviations; phase rows are outcome-unit contrasts.
- ci_low / ci_high: approximate 95% interval for Beta. Direct ordinal Wald intervals are diagnostic; direct significance uses the bootstrap test, not exclusion of zero in this interval.
- p: see p_method. Direct ordinal interactions use null-bootstrap upper bounds and BH across two tests. Secondary AR1/ARMA20 rows use CR2/Satterthwaite p-values and BH per term across 26 outcomes within each equation; no correction spans all 12 secondary alternatives. Independent fallback p-values are withheld.
- FDR_significant: TRUE if q < 0.05; FALSE if a computed q is >= 0.05; blank if no valid q was computed. Blank does not mean a tested negative result.
- q_note: why q is blank (fallback fit, not tested, or derived).
- covariance_robust: TRUE if Beta stays similar when the error-correlation assumptions are changed; FALSE means the result depends on those assumptions and must not be called robust.
- residual_structure: AR1 = standard model allowing correlation between consecutive 10-s windows; ARMA20 = two-lag correlation, used when AR1 rho > 0.95; independent_fallback = simpler model used only when AR1 failed (not used for significance).
- n: number of 10-second windows in the model. model_note: technical warnings for that model (fallback, near-zero random-effect variance, very high autocorrelation).

## Equation design and rationale
Twelve exploratory equations: four heat representations x three separate PM representations. Distinct radiation/light measurements, phase and exertion are retained; heat x sound and MRT x sound are included.
Rationale: thermal indices (PET, UTCI, HUMIDEX) are computed from air temperature, humidity, wind and radiation/MRT and are strongly inter-correlated, so they are not co-entered.
- Zhu et al. 2024 (H1 to H4/H4 - Zhu et al 2024 ...pdf, p.5): "the high correlation among components may lead to multicollinearity".
- Weisskopf, Seals & Webster 2018, Environ Health Perspect 126:067003 (H1 to H4/H4 - Weisskopf Seals Webster 2018 ...xml): including highly correlated co-exposures in one model can amplify bias.
- Biel et al. 2020 (H1 to H4/H4 - Biel et al 2020 ...pdf, p.9): related exposure metrics were analysed "in separate models".
- Supervision meeting 2026-09-30, s6.4: "do not put PET + UTCI + MRT + all of their component variables into one model without a deliberate non-redundant selection strategy".
No located source prescribes this exact candidate-equation set; it is a design choice motivated by the sources above.

## Selection limitations requiring review
Step 4 retains candidates without PCA/LASSO. Separate PM and heat representations avoid the flagged combinations; complete-design VIF/rank still require review.
Low VIF does not establish a valid adjustment set, causal interpretation or adequate power. Per-equation FDR does not control a combined family over all twelve alternatives; findings are exploratory.

## PhaseID vs single continuous predictors

PhaseID enters as **three** sum-to-zero contrasts (`PhaseSumWalkG`, `PhaseSumWalkU`, `PhaseSumBikeG`; BikeU implicit).
In `car::vif`, that span is one **Df = 3** term with a generalized VIF (GVIF).
- **GVIF^(1/(2*Df))** is what `car::vif` prints for multi-df terms (Fox & Monette 1992; [car::vif](https://rdrr.io/cran/car/man/vif.html)).
- **VIF_equivalent = GVIF^(1/Df)** is the square of that root; we compare **VIF_equivalent** to 
5
 for the PhaseID block.
- Continuous **Df = 1** columns use ordinary VIF (= VIF_equivalent). Values above the threshold are **flagged only**; no predictor is dropped.
