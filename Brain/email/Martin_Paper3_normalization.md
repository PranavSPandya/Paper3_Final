I hope you are doing well. I checked the duration and normalization results, and I had these questions.
1.	For matched vs. full duration, the overall results are unchanged after FDR correction. I am considering proceeding with only the matched 8-minute data across all phases (Walking Urban, Biking Urban, Walking Green, and Biking Green) for the primary analysis.
2.	For normalization, as per our discussion I first tried subtracting the indoor and then the tram reference values from all phases. The indoor baseline cannot be applied consistently because eye-tracking was not recorded during the indoor phase. Tram subtraction yielded 186 models significant after FDR correction, but I am hesitant to use tram as the reference because the conditions were not constant; the air conditioning was sometimes not working and the tram became quite hot. I found previous studies used within-person Z-score standardization Wang et al. (2019) and Tervonen et al. (2023). This method does not require the indoor or tram phase as a baseline. With this approach, 320 models remained significant after FDR correction. Would it be acceptable to use this approach?
3.	For H2 (concurrence between physiological and psychological responses), I used a person-centred mixed-effects model: wpz(Physiology) ~ Mode + wpz(Psychological measure) + (1 | ParticipantID). Similar within-person psychological–physiological coherence has been examined using multilevel/mixed-effects approaches by Van Doren et al. (2021) and Cuve et al. (2023). In the matched 8-minute analysis, eight relationships remained significant after FDR correction, mainly involving PRS, pleasantness, anxiety, and eye-tracking measures. Would this be an appropriate way to test psychological–physiological concurrence?
