The European style of education gives students a lot of freedom, but it also expects a lot of self-discipline in return, which can be quite confusing for non-Europeans. I struggled with this "help yourself" + "fly or die" situation during my Master's too, so I understand how it feels.
When I start on a new research topic, this is roughly how I work:
•	Review the literature to see what previous studies did.
•	Follow their methods closely, reproducing their process, and look for gaps that haven't been addressed yet.
•	When I run into an issue, check whether earlier studies faced the same problem and how they handled it.
•	Share my trace of thoughts with the professor and adjust the direction of research based on professor's comments.
•	Repeat the cycle, always starting from the questions that were left open last time.
Over the past few weeks, it seems we have been going around in circles on the four hypotheses, with a different issue coming up each time. I noticed something similar with the green routes in the experiment and with the mixed linear model in the previous paper. I think a more structured workflow would help you a lot here, and it's also one of the key skills a PhD is meant to build.
In our last discussion, a few basic points about the analysis weren't clear yet, for example how solar radiation was processed (Gh/Go), and whether you are predicting psychological responses from physiological variables or the other way around. It would also be worth checking whether other studies have predicted a physiological variable from a single environmental factor. If none have, it's probably not the standard approach.
