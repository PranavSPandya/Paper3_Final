# H4 source methods and local applications

The sources below supply methodological precedents, not five exact replications. Local implementations change measurement, outcome, timing or scope and are described as **source-informed adaptations**. Each source keeps its own published decision rule at nominal *p*; **no BH/FDR correction is applied anywhere in H4**, because imposing a shared correction family would change every source's stated method. A separate present-study model also tests the dBA×UTCI coefficient as a labelled sensitivity check. **How H4 is defined in this study — following the sources' own claim language of cross-modal effects rather than a product-term framing — is stated at the end of this document.** These specifications were developed after inspecting this dataset.

Local differences include chamber-to-route contrasts (Chiucchiù), changed trip-summary/outcome/scope and temperature-SD specifications (Rodrigues), and a cross-correlation latency proxy (Grapas). Cureau’s local acoustic-comfort and overall-comfort items also differ from the source acoustic-quality and five-category overall items. A paired Wilcoxon procedure is appropriate to the local paired observations but is an explicit replacement for the source Mann–Whitney test, not a replication.

Six papers previously listed here are **excluded** because they do not test thermal and acoustic influence on comfort. Their PDFs are prefixed `Excluded` in `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/`. Reasons: Pigliautile 2021 — no test (bar charts only); Cureau 2023 — authors stated that no statistical test was performed; Kraus 2013 — sound only, no thermal variable; Ueberham 2019 — perceived exposure intensity, not comfort; Korpilo 2024 — soundscape quality → restoration, not discomfort (reversing restoration is a construct substitution, not an H4 test); Moshammer 2019 — noise and air pollution on HRV, temperature as a weather covariate, not thermal–acoustic discomfort (no comfort votes).

1. **Grapas C., Llaguno-Munitxa M., Pisello A. L., Irajpour A.** — *Christos Grapas, Brussels.* Urban chronotypes as climate vulnerability indicators: safety-modulated bioclimatic responses and adaptive behavior across diurnal urban contexts. *Urban Climate* 2026;67:102906. Brussels, Belgium. 18 participants, 36 walk sessions, day/night × high/low safety strata. Wearable sound (dBA), UTCI, visual exposure, air quality, Empatica physiology, eye-tracking, GPS behaviour. **Methodological precedent; local application is adapted.**
    - **Hypothesis statement (verbatim):** <mark>"First (H1), the study aims to answer whether threat perception modulates the strength of directed pathways linking environmental stimuli to behavioral responses via physiological states."</mark> — <mark>"Second (H2), we examine whether environmental domains show differential context-sensitivity: if some domains (e.g., thermal) reorganize across conditions while others (e.g., air quality) remain stable, this would indicate domain-specific versus universal processing mechanisms."</mark>
    - *Local PDF:* `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/Included - Sound - Grapas2026 - Urban chronotypes climate vulnerability safety-modulated bioclimatic responses.pdf`
    - *Bootstrap pathway strength* — indirect path coefficients in the stimulus–organism–response network, resampled; the source reports bootstrap confidence intervals rather than a test statistic.
    - *Granger-style latency* — directed lag comparison between exposure series and response series; the outcome is a delay in seconds/minutes, not a slope.
    - *Mixed-effects model on response delays* — `delay ~ exposure + (1 | participant)`, with random effects absorbing between-person variation in baseline delay.
    - *No comfort-vote model.* Sound and thermal are never entered as a product.

2. **Chiucchiù A., Gnecco V. M., Pigliautile I., Pisello A. L.** — *Anna Laura / Pisello network.* The role of personality in multi-domain environmental perception: an occupant-centric approach. *Building and Environment* 2026;290:114140. Perugia, Italy. NEXT.ROOM chamber, 25 participants, 21 °C (C0) → 23 °C (C1) crossed with 7 randomised soundscapes. Empatica E4 at 1-min cadence; PT100 / Delta OHM room sensors; IPIP-NEO-120. **Methodological precedent; local application is adapted.**
    - **Hypothesis statement (verbatim):** <mark>"This study investigates cross-domain effects between thermal and acoustic environments, with a specific focus on how personality traits modulate perceptual responses."</mark> — <mark>"Accordingly, this study aims to investigate the cross-modal influence of acoustic and thermal comfort on occupants' perception of the indoor environment, considering individual personality traits."</mark> — Research question: <mark>"Do personality-driven differences influence occupants' perception of indoor conditions, particularly thermal and acoustic, within a multi-domain comfort framework?"</mark>
    - *Local PDF:* `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/Included - Sound - Chiucchiu2026 - personality multi-domain environmental perception NEXT.ROOM.pdf`
    - *Test 1 — paired t-test with Cohen's d_z on tonic EDA, heart rate and skin temperature, C1 vs C0.* Parameters: α = 0.05, n = 25, paired by participant. Formula: $d_i = Y_{i,C1} - Y_{i,C0}$; $t = \bar{d} / (s_d/\sqrt{n})$ with df = n − 1; effect size $d_z = \bar{d}/s_d$, *"These comparisons were tested for statistical significance using paired t-test, and effect size were quantified using Cohen's d for paired samples, computed as the mean difference divided by the standard deviation of the paired differences"*.
    - *Test 2 — Spearman rank correlation among perceptual domains* (thermal vs acoustic, thermal vs pleasantness, acoustic vs pleasantness). Parameters: α = 0.05, chosen because 11 of 15 variables were non-normal on Shapiro–Wilk (*p* < 0.05). Formula: $\rho_S = 1 - 6\sum d_k^2 / [n(n^2-1)]$.
    - *Test 3 — Table 5 delta per soundscape × descriptor.* Parameters: 7 soundscapes × 8 descriptors; asterisk where *p* < 0.05. Formula: $\Delta_{s,k} = \bar{Y}_{s,k,C1} - \bar{Y}_{s,k,C0}$.
    - *Not tested:* the "crossed effect" in §3.4 is a visual radar-chart comparison between clusters. No interaction term, no test statistic.

3. **Cureau R. J., Pigliautile I., Kousis I., Pisello A. L.** — *Anna Laura / Pisello network.* Multi-domain human-oriented approach to evaluate human comfort in outdoor environments. *International Journal of Biometeorology* 2022;66:2033–2045. Perugia, Italy. Two urban parks, walk from Park 1 to Park 2 repeated twice daily (14:00 and 18:00) on four consecutive days in June, three participants per walk, about 75 min each; plus local park users surveyed on site. Backpack wearable (GPS, air temperature, RH, wind, radiation, illuminance, CO₂, PM); C.A. 832 digital sonometer at response time; fixed weather station. **Contextual source** — the local vote–vote and paired comparisons are adaptations. The source also fits environmental multinomial models including measured sound pressure; these do not supply the identical local interaction model.
    - **Hypothesis statement (verbatim):** <mark>"This paper aimed to evaluate human comfort in parks, verifying the importance of using a multi-domain (simultaneously evaluating thermal, visual, acoustic, and air quality) and multi-disciplinary (combining environmental and social fields) approach."</mark>
    - *Local PDF:* `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/Included - Sound - Cureau2022 - multi-domain human-oriented comfort outdoor environments.pdf` (supplement: `Experiment papers/Sound_evidence/Cureau2022_supplement_questionnaire_tables.pdf`)
    - *Test 1 — Mann–Whitney U* comparing the two parks on environmental parameters and on survey votes. Parameters: nine environmental parameters × seven walks = 63 tests, α = 0.05. Formula: $U = n_1 n_2 + n_1(n_1+1)/2 - R_1$.
    - *Test 2 — Fisher exact test* on contingency tables of comfort-domain answer categories, computed separately per park. Parameters: α = 0.05, both participants' and local people's responses pooled. Park 1 results: thermal sensation *p* = 0.045, visual sensation *p* = 0.016, acoustic quality *p* < 0.001, noise perception *p* = 0.101. Park 2: thermal sensation *p* = 0.075, visual sensation *p* = 0.168, noise perception *p* = 0.291, acoustic quality *p* = 0.083, acoustic appropriateness *p* = 0.419. Formula (2×2): $P = \binom{a+b}{a}\binom{c+d}{c} / \binom{n}{a+c}$, generalised by summing hypergeometric probabilities.
    - *Test 3 — six multinomial logistic regressions per park* predicting the 5-point overall comfort vote, each with a different variable set, compared by McFadden pseudo-*R*², with chi-square tests on the independent variables. Formula: $\ln[P(Y_i=j)/P(Y_i=J)] = \alpha_j + \mathbf{x}_i'\boldsymbol{\beta}_j$, *j* = 1,…,*J*−1.
    - *Key parameter:* models on survey answers reached *R*² = 0.820 (Park 1) and 0.806 (Park 2); models on measured environmental data reached only 0.126 and 0.111.
    - *Critical detail:* Fisher cross-domain tests use survey votes, including acoustic quality. Measured sound pressure also enters environmental multinomial model 2 (Supplementary Appendix D, Tables D1/D3). Local acoustic comfort is not the source acoustic-quality item.

4. **Rodrigues A. L., da Conceição M. S. A., Ramusga R. A. L., Azevedo C. L., Morgado P.** The influence of urban context on emotions and bodily responses during walking. *Journal of Urban Health* 2026;103:344–356. Lisbon, Portugal. 90 residents, 2,207 walking trips, August–December 2024. Empatica E4 (HR, RMSSD, EDA, skin temperature; HRV in 10-s windows); Lden noise map in a 25 m buffer (mean/min/max/SD); IPMA daily weather. **Methodological precedent; local application is adapted.**
    - **Hypothesis statement (verbatim):** <mark>"Second, we expect that environmental stressors like noise, heat, and technical urban infrastructure are linked to heightened physiological activation, either through increased variability, peak responses, or reduced parasympathetic regulation (H2)."</mark> — Conclusion: <mark>"Mixed-effects models (Table 2; Supplementary Table S4) further confirmed that heat and noise were the strongest predictors of physiological stress."</mark>
    - *Local PDF:* `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/Included - Sound - Rodrigues2026 - Repeated hotspot exposures Lisbon walks LMM.pdf`
    - *Trip-level mixed models* fitted with Python `statsmodels` `MixedLM`. Parameters: α = 0.05; participant random intercept; all predictors standardised before fitting; trip retained if ≥ 2 min of valid sensor contact; 27 physiological indicators across HRV, EDA and skin temperature.
    - *Five separate thematic blocks:* Noise, Temperature, Technical Infrastructure, Functional POIs, Visual Elements — **each block tested in its own model**, never combined.
    - *Noise block formula:* $Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 z(\text{NOISE}_{\max,ij}) + \beta_2 z(\text{NOISE}_{SD,ij}) + u_{0i} + \varepsilon_{ij}$.
    - *Temperature block formula:* $Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 z(T_{\max,ij}) + \beta_2 z(T_{\mathrm{mean},ij}) + u_{0i} + \varepsilon_{ij}$.
    - *Retention rule:* *p* < 0.05 **and** |β| > 0.1, both required.
    - *Verified code structure:* the authors’ `7.Model_H2.py` fits separate noise and temperature blocks; its temperature predictors are `Temp_max` and `Temp_mean`. This is a code observation, not a verbatim quotation from the paper.

5. **Peng Z., Lugten M., Wuite G., Luo W., Santucci D.** Coping with urban heat and noise: a case study at Schiphol Airport. *12th International Conference on Urban Climate* (ICUC-12), abstract ICUC12-816, 2025. https://doi.org/10.5194/icuc12-816. Schiphol, Netherlands. 24 student participants, 23 July 2024, three courtyard walking transects (stop-and-go, 3-minute intervals, 60 minutes). Skin-temperature iButtons, Polar heart-rate wristbands, right-here-right-now TSV/ASV questionnaires; site UTCI and LAeq from Kestrel trackers and Munisense microphones. **Methodological precedent for the updated wording; not a product-term test of the original H4.** Local application is adapted.
    - **Hypothesis statement (from abstract):** mixed-linear regression tested five hypotheses on TSV, ASV, UTCI and LAeq. One rejected: heat stress and aircraft noise do not confound acoustical perception. Two fully supported: aircraft noise can confound thermal perceptions; acoustical perception can confound thermal perception.
    - *Local PDF:* `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/NeedScreen - Sound - Peng2025 - Schiphol urban heat noise walking ICUC12 abstract.pdf`
    - *Five mixed-linear models (walk = source design; bike = labelled extension):* `TSV ~ LAeq`; `TSV ~ ASV`; `ASV ~ UTCI`; `ASV ~ LAeq`; `ASV ~ TSV`, each with a participant random intercept. Coefficients were not published in the one-page abstract.
    - *Not tested:* a UTCI × LAeq product on discomfort.

6. **Grapas C., Schweiker M., Pisello A. L., Pigliautile I., Cureau R. J.** Do we all feel the city the same way? A proof-of-concept framework linking physiological wellbeing to neighborhood-scale resilience through multi-domain environmental sensing. *Building and Environment* 2026;300:114679. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2026.114679. Rome, Italy. Eight participants, 1–2 July 2024, one-hour comfort walks (~2.5 km) with Empatica heart rate and EDA plus backpack UTCI, PM, CO₂ and acoustic spectrum. **Methodological precedent for multi-domain physiology, not a product-term test of the original H4.** Local application is adapted.
    - **Hypothesis statement (from the paper):** multi-domain models combining thermal, acoustic and air-quality parameters explained up to 43% of physiological variance; UTCI alone produced no predictive power. Strongest published pair is UTCI–PM.
    - *Local PDF:* `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/NeedScreen - Sound - Grapas2026 - Rome feel the city same way physiological wellbeing.pdf`
    - *Test 1 — Pearson or Spearman of heart rate and tonic EDA with UTCI, dBA, PM2.5 and octave-band shares* (source §2.3; |*r*| > 0.2 then *p* < 0.05). Walk = source design; bike = labelled extension.
    - *Test 2 — linear analogue of the Richards pairwise model:* `Y ~ UTCI_z + dBA_z + (1 | participant)` on bout cells. The published object is a generalized logistic growth surface on time series; that nls is not fitted on 22 bout means.
    - *Not tested:* a UTCI × LAeq product on discomfort votes.

7. **Baquero Larriva M. T., Higueras García E.** Factores ambientales que influyen en el uso del espacio público para las personas mayores en Madrid. *Urbano* 2019;22(40):108–126. https://doi.org/10.22320/07183607.2019.22.40.06. Madrid, Spain. 200 older adults in three public spaces, 2018. Environmental measurements plus interviews. **Methodological precedent; local dependent variable is overall comfort because there is no stay-count series.**
    - **Hypothesis statement (from the paper):** stay was associated with temperature, humidity, wind and noise, but only maximum wind entered the multiple regression (*R*² = 7%).
    - *Local PDF:* `Experiment papers/H4 Sound literature test/NeedScreen - Sound - Baquero2020 - Madrid environmental factors older adults Spanish.pdf`
    - *Pearson:* `overall_comfort ~ Ta`, `~ RH`, `~ dBA`, `~ wind`.
    - *Spearman:* `overall_comfort ~ thermal_comfort`, `~ acoustic_comfort` (source correlated stay with thermal sensation and noise perception).
    - *Multiple regression:* `overall_comfort ~ Ta_z + RH_z + dBA_z + wind_z + (1 | participant)`.
    - *Not tested:* occupancy counts, and no thermal × sound product.

Newcastle 2020 (Baquero & Higueras, short communication) and Zouad & Baron 2026 (France thermal walks) were read and not ported: no inferential thermal-and-sound test.

## H4 statement

**Previous wording:**

> H4: exposure to sound amplifies discomfort under thermally uncomfortable outdoor conditions.

**Updated wording:**

> H4: Measured thermal exposure (UTCI) and sound level (dBA) are each associated with reported comfort during outdoor walking and cycling.

These are not the same claim. The updated sentence is both measured exposures on reported comfort. Cureau (2022) models ratings together; Peng (2025) fits separate one-predictor models. Neither is a `dBA × UTCI` product. No screened European outdoor study estimated amplification with a sound × thermal product on discomfort. "And cycling" is this campaign's extension; the European outdoor precedents are walking. The dBA × UTCI product remains the original-H4 test.
