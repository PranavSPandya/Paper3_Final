## Follow-up analyses the existing data support

1. **Route contrast on common wind support.** Compare urban and green dB(A) and spectral summaries within each mode, first using all bouts and then restricting observations to the wind range shared by both routes. Summarize differences at participant level. Report how many rows and participants remain. This directly tests whether wind conditions matter for the apparent exposure contrast.
2. **Separate speed from apparent wind as far as the data allow.** The exploratory check already fitted dB(A) on apparent wind and GPS speed separately for each participant and route–mode bout. Show the participant slopes, check how strongly the two predictors covary, and test whether the result depends on stop–start epochs. Report participant-level uncertainty if taking the estimate beyond a descriptive sensitivity analysis.
3. **Audit the audio labels.** If the source audio is available, sample high- and low-wind clips from each route and mode and annotate audible wind and traffic blind to the model result. This tests whether automated labels and low-frequency proportions track recognizable sounds. A stationary reference or paired microphones would still be needed to quantify recording artefact.
4. **Keep comfort inference at bout level.** Compare the sound–comfort slope before and after route adjustment and show the estimates and intervals. With 22 walking bouts and one vote per bout, the 10-second readings add exposure detail without adding independent comfort observations.

The submission angle is measurement comparability. A portable reference microphone or paired windscreens under controlled runs would be a separate validation study.
