volunteers  with  normal  hearing,  randomly  divided  into  4  equal  sized  groups,  were  tested  in
a  climatic  chamber  under  4  combinations  of  two  environmental  conditions  made  up  of  noise
and  heat,  each  at  two   levels   : 70  dB  (A)  (quiet),  100  dB  (A)   (noise)   :  28(cid:129)Ž  (comfortable),  35(cid:129)Ž
C  (heat).  A  statistically  balanced  procedure  was  followed.  Three  physiological  variables

INTRODUCTION
  Noise  and  heat,  amongst  many  other  factors,  are  concomitant  stressors  that
produce  complex  work  environment  in  shop  floors  of  industrial  processes.  Yet,
most  of  the  investigations  have  been  designed  to  understand  human  performance
efficiency  accompanied  by  noise  or  heat  independently  from  each  other.  Ham-
ilton  et   all)  found  that  noise  aided  recall  involving  order  information,  while  its
detrimental  effects  have  been  reported  by  Broadbent  and   Gregory2)  on  signal

interposed with heat on performance efficiency of psychomotor tasks at variable
difficulty levels as well as on some physiological variables as indicators of
activation level.
MATERIALS AND METHODS
Subjects : A group of 24 male college student volunteers, having sound physical
health with normal hearing, participated in this experiment. All of them wore
cotton clothes providing insulation value of 0.6 do unit8). They did not have any
previous experience of exposures to noise and heat routinely. The anthropometric
measurements of the subjects are given in Table 1.
Environmental measures : A few equipments such as audiometer (M/s. Arphi
India Ltd, India), sound level meter, Type 2218 (B & K, Denmark), and a
climatic chamber (M/s. Hotpack corporation, U. S. A.) were used. The details
of the equipments are given in a previous report by Bhattacharya et al6).
Physiological measures : Heart rate counter (Hauer, Switzerland), D eep body
thermometer (DBT Ltd., England) and sphygmomanometer (Erkameter, Germa-
ny) were used for measurements of the physiological variables6).
Heart rate counter : The heart rate of the subject was recorded by counting 10
heart beats with the help of a stethoscope (Reister, Germany) placed at the left
fifth intercostal space of his chest, noting the time from the watch dial of the
counter and then reading the appropriate scale in it7).
Deep body thermometer : A deep body thermometer was used with its electrode
taped on the chest of the subject to record body temperature ((cid:129)Ž). The remote
sensing equipment was kept outside the chamber.
Sphygmomanometer : The sphygmomanometer was kept at the level of the
heart and the cuff was tied round the upper arm. The chest piece of the
stethoscope was placed on the brachial artery. Pressure was raised to 200 mm
Hg and then gradually released. The first clear occurrence of the tapping sound
indicated systolic pressure while the just disappearance of the sound recorded
diastolic pressure.
Psychomotor measures : A battery of psychomotor tasks containing tweezer
dexterity, memory and search (letter cancellation), two hand coordination and

reaction time were used in this experiment. The details have been given by
Bhattacharya et al9,10). The scoring of the tasks were as follows :
Tweezer dexterity : The number of metallic pins inserted into the holes on
wooden boards with the help of a tweezer in 3 min. The task was in two
categories. In (cid:129)ea' category the diameter of each pin was 3.5 mm and that of each
hole 4.0 mm, while in (cid:129)eb' category the diameter of each pin and hole were 1.5
mm and 1.6 mm respectively.
Memory and sear ch task : The number of letters cancelled in an array contain-
ing 5 lines with 50 randomised letters in each line in 3 min. The three categories
of the task were cancellation of : (a) one letter, (b) three letters and (c) five
letters.
Two hand coordination : The time taken (sec) in tracing a geometrical pattern
in two categories : (a) a straight line with deflections on its left and right ends,
(b) curvatures in addition to that of (cid:129)ea'.
Reaction time : The average time (in 1/100 sec unit) taken for 30 trials to
respond to (a) one stimulus and (b) three stimuli as quickly after its onset as
possible.
In all the tests, (cid:129)ea' category represented the difficulty levels as low, (cid:129)eb' category
moderate and (cid:129)ec' category high. The higher scores indicated superior perform-
ance in tweezer dexterity and memory and search task, while lower scores
reflected better performance in two hand coordination and reaction time tests.
Design of the experiment : Based on the experience gained through surveys of
noise and thermal environment in textile industries in India the levels of sound
pressure which correspond to 70 dB (A) and 100 dB (A) and air temperatures
which correspond to 28° C and 35° C respectively were administered.
In the climatic chamber wihte noise was generated by t he audiometer, amplified
through a set of speakers and measured with the sound level meter so that the
sound pressure reached the desired level at the subject's ear.
The subjects were divided into four groups of 6 each, following a random
number table". First, under quiet condition (70 dB (A)), the subjects of group
1 worked at comfortable air temperature (28(cid:129)Ž) and on the second occasion
under not condition (35(cid:129)Ž), while those of group 2 first worked under hot
condition and on the second occasion under comfortable condition so as to
eliminate the effects resulting from practice11). Likewise, under noise condition
the subjects of group 3 first worked under comfortable air temperature and then
on another occasion under hot condition while those of group 4 worked first
under hot condition followed by comfortable condition on the second occasion.
Thus each subject was tested under two different combinations of noise and heat,
each lasting for an hour and half. The air velocity in the climatic chamber was
0.25 m/sec with the relative humidity being 50%. The thermal comfortable
range of Indian subjects wearing cotton clothes of 0.6 clo unit was assessed to lie
within a range of 23.3-28.3(cid:129)Ž12).

DISCUSSION
It has been observed that the effects of environmental noise and heat on human
performance in the form of either improvement, impairment or no change depend
on the task, the particular combination of environmental conditions and the
length of exposure13). The psychological and physiological variables studied in
this investigation were based on the recommendation of ILO dealing with
behavioural disturbances due to exposure to noise and heat15).
The significant F-values for the independent effects of noise and heat on heart
rate suggest that noise singly caused elevations to 83.50 beats/min from 78.01
beats/min and heat to 83.95 beats/min from 78.25 beats/min. On blood press ure
jsystolic), Fig. 1 illustrates appreciable interaction effect of noise and heat
signifying that under quiet condition blood pressure increased with the rise in air
temperature, which was depressed by noise in the presence of heat. Body
temperature was affected by heat rising to 37.12(cid:129)Ž at air temperature of 35(cid:129)Ž
from 36.48(cid:129)Ž at comfortable condition. The combined effect of noise and heat

The ind ependent effect of noise on (cid:129)ea' category of two-hand coordination task

acted as a dearousing stressoe lowering efficiency in coordinating the two hands
of the subjects in tracing a less difficult geometrical pattern containing a straight
line with deflections on its two ends. The interaction effect (Fig. 2b) reflected
that performance deteriorated at high heat of 35(cid:129)Ž under quiet condition, which
improved under noise condition at raised air temperature of 35(cid:129)Ž. As this
category of the task was relatively difficult, noise in combination with heat
possibly enhanced the arousal level of the subjects allowing higher order mental
input and thereby resulting in favcurable performance.
Further in (cid:129)ea' category reaction time w as longer (mean 35.50) under noise
condition compared to that (mean 31.89) in quiet condition, while in (cid:129)eb' catego-
ry, a reverse trend of shorter reaction time was observable, with the mean values
being 74.98 and 76.54 respectively. Also, in (cid:129)ea' category the heat prolonged the
reaction time to the mean value of 35.57 from the mean value of 31.84 at
comfortable condition, but in (cid:129)eb' category reaction time was shorter to 74.42 at
35(cid:129)Ž from 78.10 at 28(cid:129)Ž. The reason for appearance of this phenomena was
that noise or heat was able to produce arousal level in the subjects high enough
to effect favourable performance changes on the more difficult task which is in
agreement with the welford's20) concept of arousal and task demand. Fig. 2c,
illustrates that in 'b' category heat caused performance improvement under both
quiet and noise conditions, but in the latter condition higher degree of improve-
ment was noticeable. This shows that heat accompanied by noise raised the
arousal level of the subjects, yielding superior performance effects.
It may thus be summarised that noise and heat caused eleva tions in heart rate
and heat escalated the body temperature. The interaction effect of noise and heat
also increased blood pressure (systolic) and body temperature. Adverse effects of
noise was observable on tweezer dexterity task with higher degradation being at
its moderate level, as well as on two hand coordination task at low difficulty
level, while that of heat on memory and search task at moderate difficulty level.
Noise and heat independently increased reaction time at low difficulty level but
