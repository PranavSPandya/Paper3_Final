1. Introduction
Thermal comfort significantly affects people’s willingness to engage in outdoor activities and the quality of urban spaces. Un-
derstanding urban outdoor thermal comfort (OTC) conditions is necessary for building a comfortable, healthy, and livable city. The
OTC comprises two primary components: the objective microclimate condition and subjective human thermal perception
(Nikolopoulou and Steemers, 2003). Both are affected by urban built environments (Nikolopoulou et al., 2001).
On the one hand, many studies have been conducted on outdoor microclimates and how they affect people’s thermal comfort.
Scholars have explored the contribution of various meteorological parameters on OTC, such as air temperature, solar radiation, wind
and humidity (Lin et al., 2011; Ng and Cheng, 2012; Li et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2022). Also, the influence of built
environment, such as urban geometry, vegetation, reflective surfaces, and waterbodies, on outdoor microclimate were investigated
(Abd Elraouf et al., 2022; Taleghani, 2018; Lai et al., 2019; Mohammad et al., 2021). On the other hand, even under similar climatic
conditions, differences in human body’s thermal perception can cause significant discrepancies in thermal comfort (Nikolopoulou and

In different regions of the world, climatic conditions vary greatly, and even within the same region, there can be significant
seasonal climate differences. Studies on thermal comfort in different climatic zones sometimes produce different, or even contra-
dictory, conclusions. Many studies have proved that people have distinctive range of comfortable temperature (Salata et al., 2016;
Hadianpour et al., 2018; Cohen et al., 2019; Yin et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023) and thermal preference (Johansson et al., 2017; Tian
et al., 2022) under various regions and seasons, which is caused by long-term thermal adaptation. The impact of various human factors
on thermal comfort also varies in the conclusions of different studies (Lai et al., 2020a). Also, the influential meteorological parameters
on OTC may vary in distinct climate conditions. For example, some studies have indicated that relative humidity is not correlated with
OTC (Li et al., 2016; Zhu et al., 2022; Kumar and Sharma, 2022), whereas others have contrasting conclusions (Chow et al., 2016; Yuan
et al., 2023). Whether wind speed can significantly affect thermal comfort has led to different conclusions in studies across various
places, with some findings suggesting that wind speed is more influential in cold climates (Liu et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2022; Xie et al.,
2022; Tian et al., 2022).
Therefore, research conclusions of this field are typically distinctive in different regions, which demonstrates the importance of
targeted OTC research based on local climate regions. However, most existing studies have focused on hot climate regions. Few have
been conducted on China’s cold winter-hot summer climate to explore the OTC seasonal differences, and comprehensively investigated
the thermal preference, influential climatic and human parameters on OTC during distinct seasons within this climatic region
(Table 1).

Thus, this study presented two research questions:
1) to clarify the seasonal OTC difference in Beijing, a city with cold winters and hot summers, including an analysis of the thermal

2) to investigate the differences in activity patterns, the presence of people, and thermal comfort between different types of urban

This study initially collected data on microclimate conditions and human thermal perception in Beijing’s urban public spaces
through a questionnaire survey and on-site microclimate measurements. Additionally, it analyzed the daily temporal distribution of
people in different types of public spaces using on-site observations. Following this, the study explored the conditions of OTC and their
seasonal variations through detailed data analysis. It also investigated the meteorological and human factors affecting OTC under the
local climatic conditions. The findings presented in this work aim to enrich the body of research on OTC in the cold winter and hot
summer regions of China, and provide theoretical insights for OTC studies, as well as practical recommendations for urban design
practices.
2. Methodology
2.1. Study area and climate condition
This study was conducted in Beijing, a metropolitan located in northern China (39.9◦N, 116.4◦E) with over 20 million population.
Beijing belongs to Dwa (humid continental climate) zone in Ko¨ppen-Geiger climate classification. The climate is characterized by hot
humid summer and cold dry winter, which may cause substantial discrepancy on human thermal perception in two seasons. Fig. 1
indicates Beijing’s average meteorological data from 2003-2022, which was obtained from the website of National Meteorological

2.2. Field study
On-site investigations, including microclimate measurement, questionnaire survey, and photo recording, were conducted in this
study to obtain OTC data.
2.2.1. Selection of public space
Seven public spaces in Beijing were selected for the field investigation. The nearest location was approximately 4 km from the city
center, whereas the farthest distance was 15 km. To investigate the differences in the physiological, psychological, and activity
characteristics of users in different types of public spaces, the seven sites were divided into three types according to their surrounding
urban land use: residential area (sites 1/2/3), office area (sites 4/5/6), and mixed area (site 7), as shown in Fig. 2a. The reasons for
selecting these sites are as follows. Firstly, all sites were densely populated and easily accessible, ensuring an adequate number of
questionnaires and the reliability of time-varying population data. Secondly, the sites were located in different areas of Beijing,
ensuring diversity in the characteristics of the site users. Lastly, all sites had a certain scale, which ensured a variety of activities for the
users.
2.2.2. On-site microclimate measurements
Two portable microclimate stations were used in this study to collect the weather data in each public space during on-site in-
vestigations (Fig. 2b). A Misol hp2550 weather station was used to capture air temperature (Ta), relative humidity (RH), and wind
speed (Va) data. A Jianda Renke 50mm diameter globe temperature sensor was used to obtain globe temperature (Tg). All sensors were
fixed at approximately 1.5 m height above the ground by tripods. All meteorological parameters were recorded in 5min interval. The
location of sensors at each public space is illustrated in Table 2. Beside air temperature and solar radiation, ground reflectance and
wind speed are significant factors influencing OTC. Therefore, all measurement points were located in well-ventilated areas, with the
gray hard ground pavement.
2.2.3. Questionnaire survey
The objective of the questionnaire survey was to obtain information on citizens’ body characteristics, activities, and thermal
perceptions. Citizens participating in free activities at the study sites were asked to complete questionnaires, whereas meteorological
parameters were measured. Each questionnaire was recorded with the time of completion to facilitate matching with the results of
microclimate measurements. All the respondents were located within 10 m of the measuring instrument and were guaranteed the same
sunlight conditions as the sensors (Fig. 2c). Additionally, when the solar radiation conditions at the measurement points changed (due
to shadows cast by buildings or trees), the questionnaire survey was paused for 10 minutes. It was resumed once the measurement
values stabilized.
The questionnaire was designed according to previous studies (Ng and Cheng, 2012; Chen et al., 2015; Shooshtarian and Ridley,
2016; Lai et al., 2020b; Tian et al., 2022) and divided into two parts. The first part focused on human factors that may influence
thermal perception, including the respondents’ physiological conditions, such as gender, age, long-term thermal experience (length of
residence in Beijing), short-term thermal experience (whether in an air-conditioned place in the past 15 min), and type of activity. It
also examined psychological factors, such as the purpose of activity, the length of stay in that place, whether they stayed alone or were

2.2.4. Human observation in public spaces
In addition to microclimate measurements and questionnaire surveys, changes in the number of users of public spaces over time
were recorded to study the temporal distribution of people in different seasons and types of public spaces. We chose to take photos of
all users of the public space three times every hour (e.g., 9:10, 9:30, and 9:50). The total number of people in the photos was accounted.
Then, the number of people at the three time points was averaged to calculate the average number of public space users per hour.
Additionally, this study examined the age distribution characteristics of users in different types of public spaces. By observing pho-
tographs, we recorded the number of elderly individuals in each photo and calculated the total number of elderly individuals present in
the public space throughout the day. Then the proportion of elderly individuals relative to the total number of users was determined. It
is important to note that, since it was not possible to accurately determine age through observation, the proportion of elderly in-
dividuals obtained by this method was compared with the proportion of respondents over 55 years old from the questionnaire to ensure
the validity of the conclusions.
2.2.5. Field survey schedule
Twenty-one field surveys were conducted during winter (11 times) and summer (10 times). The dates, times and measured weather
conditions of field survey is listed in Table 3. To study the temporal distribution of users in public spaces at different times, one
weekday and one weekend were selected for investigating residential and mixed-use public spaces. As offices are used less frequently
on weekends, on-site investigations were conducted only on weekdays in each office public space. None of the investigations were
conducted under rainy or snowy days.

3. Results and data analysis
During the field survey, 986 questionnaires were given out during the survey campaign and 975 valid questionnaires were collected
(494 in summer and 481 in winter). A total number of 21379 people were recorded (10871 in summer and 10508 in winter). During
microclimate measurement, Ta was recorded between 22.2◦C to 38.3◦C in summer and -4.3◦C to 12.4◦C in winter, RH was recorded
between 22% to 87% in summer and 16% to 75% in winter, Va was recorded between 0m/s to 2.8m/s in summer and 0m/s to 2.1m/s in
winter, Tg was recorded between 23.8◦C to 48.5◦C in summer and -2.6◦C to 19.2◦C in winter, PET was calculated between 22.8◦C to
55.2◦C in summer and -4.1◦C to 20.2◦C in winter.
3.1. Overall thermal perception in two seasons
Table 4shows the proportion of each TSV scale during the two seasons, based on the questionnaire survey. During summer, 33% of
respondents chose “neutral”, while during winter, 46.6% chose this option. 36.8% of respondents chose “slightly hot” during summer,
while 41.2% chose “slightly cold” during winter. Meanwhile, 29.7% of respondents chose “hot” and “very hot” during summer, while
only 8.5% chose “cold” and “very cold” during winter. The average TSV was 1.06 for summer with the standard deviation of 0.97. In
contrast, the average TSV was -0.55 for winter, with the standard deviation of 0.73. Therefore, it can be concluded that there are
considerable differences in thermal perception between summer and winter in Beijing. People are generally more tolerant to cold
weather during winter than to hot weather during summer. This hypothesis was also supported by the comparison of TCV between the
two seasons (Table 5). During summer, 34% of respondents chose “comfortable”, which was 12.4% less than during winter. Mean-
while, 8.1% of respondents chose “unacceptable” during summer, and only 2.7% of respondents chose this option during winter.
3.2. Locating NPET, NPETR and TAR
The relationship between the measured PET and mean TSV (MTSV) was examined. MTSV was calculated at an interval of 1◦C PET
bin separately for summer and winter, and the regression model were calculated and shown in Fig. 3. The neutral temperature refers to
the temperature at which people feel neither cold nor hot (ASHRAE, 2017). According to past studies (Cheung and Jim, 2017; Tian
et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2023), neutral PET (NPET) is defined as MTSV=0, and neutral PET range (NPETR) is defined as MTSV between
-0.5 and 0.5. People have the most comfortable thermal perceptions in this range. By the regression model that was developed in this
study, the NPET of residents in Beijing were 21.9◦C for summer and 14.3◦C for winter, while the NPETR were 15.2–28.6◦C for summer
and 6.1–22.5◦C for winter. Thus, NPET has substantial differences for the two seasons in Beijing, which reflects the discrepancy in
thermal adaptation and thermal expectations of residents in Beijing, a cold-winter and hot-summer climate region. Same method was
applied to obtain the NPET for public spaces near residential area and office area. The NPETs were 18.7◦C (R2=0.227, Sig.=0.00) and
26.8◦C (R2=0.530, Sig.=0.01) in summer, and 13.1◦C (R2=0.645, Sig.=0.00) and 15.3◦C (R2=0.392, Sig.=0.00) in winter for resi-
dential and office area respectively.

3.3. Meteorological factors that affect TSV
The dominant influence of climatic factors on OTC, such as Tg, Ta, RH, and Va, has been proven. However, the perceptions of each
microclimatic factor vary under different climatic conditions (Lai et al., 2014; Deng et al., 2023). Therefore, this study obtained the
weights of various microclimatic factors affecting the thermal perceptions of residents for Beijing’s winter and summer. An ordinal
logistic regression model was used to analyze the influence of each climatic factor on the TSV. Table 6indicates the correlation be-
tween the climatic factors and TSV for two seasons. During summer, Tg, Ta, and RH were positively correlated with TSV, whereas Va
was negatively correlated with TSV. During winter, Tg was positively correlated with TSV, whereas Va was negatively correlated with
TSV. However, Ta and RH did not significantly influence TSV. Stepwise logistic regression was conducted to determine the weight of
influence of each factor. The sequence of influential weights on TSV was Tg >RH>Ta >Va and Tg>Va during summer and winter,
respectively.
Fig. 5shows the percentages of thermal discomfort and preference votes during the two seasons. During winter (Fig. 5a), 20% of the
respondents felt that sunlight was too weak, 26% thought that high wind speed caused discomfort, and 24% felt disturbed by low

4. Discussion
4.1. NPET Comparison with other regions
This study found that the NPET of Beijing area was 21.9 ◦ C for summer and 14.3 ◦ C for winter. The NPETR was 15.2–28.6 ◦ C for
summer and 6.1–22.5◦C for winter. By comparing similar studies in different climate regions (Table 8), it can be found that the NPET in
hotter climate regions are higher than Beijing, such as Taichung and Tehran, and the NPET in colder climate regions are lower than
Beijing, such as Harbin, both for summer and winter. This indicates that local residents have adaptability to the local climate.
Moreover, based on the comparison, it can be concluded that there are huge NPET differences between summer and winter (ΔPET) in
areas with distinct seasons, such as Beijing (ΔPET =7.6◦C), Harbin (ΔPET =7.7◦C), Xi’an (ΔPET =6.4◦C), and Tehran (ΔPET =
7.9◦C). However, for areas with little seasonal temperature variance, such as Taichung (ΔPET =1.9◦C), Melbourne (ΔPET =4.1◦C),
2.2◦C), and Rome (ΔPET = 2.0◦C), the NPET differences between summer and winter are relatively small.

4.2. Summer cooling or winter insulation?
This study revealed that meteorological factors have a major influence on TSV in Beijing’s winter and summer. In both seasons, Tg
was most influential on TSV. Therefore, changes in sunlight conditions remain essential to improving OTC. During summer, the
importance of the meteorological parameters was ranked as Tg >RH>Ta >Va. Meanwhile during winter, the essential factors that affect
OTC are Tg and Va, whereas Ta and RH are not influential. This result is consisted to existing studies (Oliveira and Andrade, 2007; Xie
et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022). However, in regions with hot summers and cold winters, there is always a contradiction between
providing shade in summer and increasing sunlight in winter, as well as between summer ventilation and winter wind protection.
Consequently, in Beijing, which is more important: winter insulation or summer cooling?
TSV and TCV analysis reveals that the residents of Beijing are generally less tolerant of summer heat than winter cold, suggesting a
higher priority for summer cooling strategies in urban public spaces. As global warming and the urban heat island effect have raised
temperatures in metropolitan areas (Tian et al., 2023), the demand for summer cooling will continue to be higher than the demand for
winter insulation in Beijing or similar climate regions，due to global warming is irreversible in the near future. Additionally, people in
Beijing are less tolerant to high air velocity in winter than low air velocity in summer. Although the wind speed had never exceeded

4.3. Human factors that affect OTC
Compared to meteorological factors, human physiological and psychological factors are less influential. This study only obtained
three indicators that substantially impacted TSV, namely visiting purpose for summer, and activity intensity and age for winter. This
result is consistent with previous studies, that human parameters are less influential under extreme climate than moderate climate
(Yuan et al., 2023). The correlation between visiting purpose and TSV is as follows. People have poorer OTC in necessary activities,
where the autonomy of activity is low, compared to spontaneous and social activities, where the autonomy of activity is high.
However, the causal relationship between activity autonomy and thermal perception remains controversial (Lai et al., 2020a). We
inferred that the extremely hot and cold climates caused a reduction in the frequency of spontaneous and social activities. Activity
intensity, is positively correlated with TSV. Higher-intensity outdoor activities such as fast walking or exercise help improve OTC
during winter. Public space attendants tended to have higher activity intensity during winter (avg. votes =1.96) than summer (avg.
votes =1.72), which also proved thermal adaptation. During both seasons, the TSV of the elderly were closer to a neutral value than
those of the young. This may be because the elderly is less sensitive to the weather owing to their relatively slow metabolism (Baquero
and Forcada, 2022). The impact of age on OTC leads to differences in thermal comfort optimization for different types of public spaces,
which is discussed in the next section.
Although the environmental stimuli factors did not exhibit a notable correlation with TSV, people tended to have better thermal
comfort in public spaces with better scenery. This result is consistent with those of previous studies (Lam et al., 2020; Yan et al., 2023;
Du et al., 2023). However, the causal relationship between them still requires further verification, namely, whether it is the positive
environmental stimuli that enhance thermal comfort or vice versa. In order to obtain this causality, ‘scenery of this place’ votes in
summer was calculated at an interval of 1◦C PET bin, and the regression model were calculated and shown in Fig. 9. The result
indicated that PET was not correlated with summer scenery (R2=0.049, sig.=0.224). Based on this, we inferred that indeed, better
environmental stimuli lead to improved thermal comfort in Beijing’s summer climate. However, this issue merits more in-depth
research. In addition, during summer, people who just go out from air-conditioned rooms are less tolerant to outdoor heat waves,
which is consistent with some existing studies (Yang et al., 2013) but contrasts with others (Ng and Cheng, 2012; Yung et al., 2019).
The relationships between them also require further investigation.
4.4. OTC and user pattern in different types of urban public space
Field investigation results exhibited a substantial discrepancy in the temporal distribution of people during the day in different
seasons and different types of public spaces. In residential area, people tend to choose more comfortable times to perform outdoor
activities during both seasons. This demonstrates that climatic conditions have a considerable influence on people’s behavior. On the
contrast, for both seasons, people in office areas tend to use public spaces around midday, which proves that the work schedule has a
stronger influence on people’s outdoor activities than climate conditions. This phenomenon highlights the necessity of targeted OTC
optimization strategies for two types of urban public spaces. In general, sunlight protection is more important for office areas than for
residential areas in summer because outdoor spaces are more commonly used during the hottest time of the day. At this time, the solar
elevation angle is large, making it difficult for buildings to provide effective shading to outdoor spaces. Tall trees with large canopy and
higher leaf area index, can effectively provide shade, reduce Tg, and improves outdoor human thermal comfort during hot seasons
(Irmak et al., 2018; Broadbent et al., 2018; Li et al., 2023). Thus, compared to residential areas, increasing the tree coverage in public
spaces of office areas, can more effectively improve thermal comfort. Some local deciduous trees, such as pagoda trees and poplars,
provide effective summer shade without blocking winter sunlight. Moreover, although this study’s survey ended at 19:00, it can be
inferred that public spaces in residential areas may still gather a significant number of users after sunset during summer. Therefore,
providing adequate lighting is crucial for enhancing the utilization efficiency of public spaces in residential areas.
In addition to differences in time periods, the proportion of elderly individuals in residential areas is significantly higher than in
office areas. As analyzed in the previous section, elderly individuals are less sensitive to high summer temperatures and low winter
temperatures compared to younger individuals. Although the mTSV of elderly citizens were closer to neutral, lower climate sensitivity
may lead to health risks when elderly individuals are exposed to extremely hot or cold weather (Larriva and Higueras, 2020). As there
were more older attendees, residential areas need to pay more attention to extreme weather conditions than office areas. According to
Larriva and Higueras’s (2020)study, activity facilities are important factors in encouraging elderly individuals to use public spaces.
Therefore, compared to office areas, activity facilities in residential areas, such as fitness equipment, chess tables, and benches, should
be more rigorously placed in locations with favorable microclimates.
Previous studies have demonstrated that urban geometry is the major ways to change outdoor microclimates (Jamei et al., 2016;
Lai et al., 2019). Under Beijing’s climatic conditions, summer shade and cooling are more important than winter insulation, whereas
the need for winter wind protection is stronger than summer ventilation. Therefore, a compact urban layout is suggested for the Beijing
area to reduce the sky view factor and increase the building coverage ratio. A lower sky view factor contributes to summer shade and
cooling (Zhang et al., 2020; Kim and Brown, 2022), whereas a higher building coverage ratio is beneficial for reducing wind speed (Ma

4.5. Limitation of the study
The selection of respondents in this study has limitations. Firstly, in urban public spaces, the majority of people did not engage in
high-intensity activities, resulting in a smaller sample size for this group. Secondly, there were fewer child respondents because very
young children might not fully comprehend the questionnaire. Thirdly, since the survey period coincided with the ongoing COVID-19
pandemic, the number of tourists was very low, which might have affected the impact of length of stay on OTC. Finally, the survey was
conducted during the hottest and coldest periods of the year, causing some individuals sensitive to extreme weather to intentionally
reduce their outdoor time. Therefore, future research needs to select other locations and methods to conduct more targeted studies on
the influence of certain human characteristics on OTC.
5. Conclusion
Using a questionnaire survey, on-site microclimate measurements, and photo documentation, this study investigated the outdoor
thermal comfort in several public spaces in Beijing during summer and winter. The following conclusions were drawn. The founding of
this study provides theoretical support for the optimization of outdoor thermal comfort in Beijing and regions with similar climates.
1) There was a considerable OTC discrepancy between winter and summer under Beijing’s local climate conditions. The NPET is
21.9◦C for summer and 14.3◦C for winter, whereas the NPETR is 15.2–28.6◦C for summer and 6.1–22.5◦C for winter.
2) Meteorological parameters have a dominant effect on the OTC in Beijing’s climate. During summer, the sequence of influential
weights was Tg >RH>Ta >Va. During winter, the sequence of influential weights on OTC was Tg >Va, whereas RH and Ta showed no
notable impacts. This seasonal variation highlights the necessity of targeted OTC optimization for different seasons in a cold winter and
hot summer region.
3) In Beijing’s outdoor spaces, summer cooling is more important than winter insulation, whereas winter wind protection is more
essential than summer ventilation.
4) Human physiological and psychological parameters, such as age, activity intensity, and visiting purpose, have certain effects on
OTC. However, they are much less influential than meteorological parameters. Short-term thermal experiences and environmental
stimuli also have a minor impact on thermal comfort, but the relationship between them requires further study.
5) There is considerable discrepancy in the user pattern in different types of public spaces and seasons. In residential areas, people
choose suitable times for outdoor activities based on the weather. However, in office areas, the timing of people’s activities is dictated
by work schedules. This discrepancy has a significant influence on the OTC optimization in different public spaces.
6) For Beijing’s local climatic conditions, a compact urban layout with concentrated open spaces is recommended to obstruct
sunlight during summer and cold winds during winter. Planting local deciduous trees is an effective way to improve OTC, especially for
public spaces in office areas, where people are exposed to extreme solar radiation at noon during summer.
