In the last couple of decades cool roofs, i.e. roof surfaces exposed to solar radiation charac-
terized by high solar reflectance (albedo) and thermal emittance, are being intensively stud-
ied for their ability to reduce the roof temperature, which leads to a lower heat transfer into
the building. This acknowledged phenomenon produces better indoor thermal comfort condi-
tions and reduced energy  requirement for cooling, resulting in  lower greenhouse gas emis-
sions. Additionally, cool roofs are also studied and optimized for their larger scale effect as
Urban Heat  Island mitigation strategy in dense cities. In this perspective, several reflective
coatings  and materials  were used for  cool  roof applications, e.g. light  color paintings, roof
tiles  and  waterproof  membranes.  Recent  research  developments  were  also  focused  on  the
analysis of natural and sustainable materials having these properties. In particular, stone ag-
gregates were studied for their intrinsic cooling potential with varying grain size and stone
typology. Roof covered by stone aggregates can be considered as a rigid frame porous mate-
rial whose acoustic properties can lead to similar advantages to the thermal ones: (i) a small
scale  benefit,  i.e.  an  increased  sound  reduction  index  of  the  roof  with  a  consequent  lower
sound transfer into the building; (ii) a larger scale benefit, i.e. a reduction of the environmen-
tal noise thanks to the increased sound absorption coefficient. This paper reports the results
of the measurement campaigns of sound absorption and transmission loss carried out on dif-
ferent samples of loose  gravel  with  different  grain  size and thickness.  To this aim, several
experimental analyses by means of impedance tube according to the transfer function meth-
od described in ISO 10534-2 were carried out. Several comparative considerations between
acoustic  and  thermal-energy  experimental  results  represent  the  object  of  this  contribution,
with the final purpose to optimize this roof typology for its twofold potential.

1.

Introduction

Building  energy  efficiency  and  indoor  microclimate  comfort  in  constructions  have  represented
key research and innovation issues [1] during the last decades. One of the main trends in this field
consisted of the analysis and the optimization of sustainable materials responsible of multiple bene-
fits such as (i) optimization of indoor thermal comfort, (ii) improvement of acoustic performance of
buildings envelope [2], (iii) positive effects of outdoor local microclimate [3]. This latter aspect was
mainly  focused  on  the  proposal  of  new  techniques  aimed  at  reducing  local  climate  changes  phe-
nomena such as urban heat island. This phenomenon mainly consists of the increase of urban tem-
peratures during the course of the year, with respect to the colder suburban and green surrounding
areas,  less  affected  by  anthropic  activities.  An  important  role  in  increasing  such  phenomenon  is
played by greenhouse gases emissions and energy consumption in buildings  [4], that is the reason
why  many  research  and  industrial  efforts  were  dedicated  to  the  proposal  of  new  and  sustainable
materials and techniques [5] with a twofold aim, i.e. to reduce building energy requirement for cool-
ing  and  to  improve  urban  livability.  This  combined  effect  was  shown  to  be  produced  by  the  so
called  “cool  materials”  that  are  those  materials  able  to  reflect  solar  radiation  and  to  emit  thermal
energy  much  more  than  classic  construction  and  paving  materials.  Therefore,  cool  materials  are
characterized  by  high  solar  reflectance,  e.g.  more  than  40-50%,  and  high  thermal  emittance,  e.g.
more than 80% [6]. Being able to reflect solar radiation and to emit thermal energy, they are able to
stay  cooler than classic materials, e.g. dark bitumen asphalt, etc., and to produce a non-negligible
passive cooling effect in buildings and in urban areas, when applied in outdoor paving systems ex-
posed to solar radiation. For these acknowledged reasons, the passive cooling potential of cool ma-
terials was investigated through numerical dynamic simulation and experimental activities in sever-
al climate conditions around the world in summer and winter conditions [7-8]. Additionally, another
research issue consisted in the proposal and improvement of reliable techniques and procedures to
perform experimental measurements of the optic-radiative material properties, i.e. albedo/solar re-
flectance [9-10]. These latter efforts were also aimed at characterizing from the optical and thermal-
energy  point  of  view  the  performance  of  natural  aggregated  layers  typically  used  all  around  the
world  for  roof  covering  and  urban  paving.  After  acknowledging  a  non-negligible  natural  “cool”
behavior of selected local stone in previous works by the same authors [10], this paper concerns the
acoustic analysis of the performance of such high reflective gravel in the form of aggregates.

The stone aggregates layers investigated in the present research can be considered as loose gran-
ular materials, in particular as rigid frame porous media with open porosity due to the presence of
interconnected pores between the particles. The complexity of the inner structure of granular mate-
rials, i.e. pores of variable sizes and shapes, introduces a higher level of difficulty in modelling their
sound  absorption  characteristics  if  compared  with  fibrous  materials,  which  can  be  studied  with  a
number of reliable models [11]. Voronina et al have thoroughly investigated the sound absorption
properties  of  loose  granular  materials,  defining  a  model  that  takes  into  account  the  following  pa-
rameters:  porosity,  tortuosity,  specific  density  and  characteristic  particle  dimension  of  the  loose
granular mix [12-13]. This model proved to be reliable if compared with experimental data obtained
testing  several  granular  materials,  including  vermiculite,  rubber  crumbs,  perlite  and  keramsite.  A
large set of experimental data related to rubber crumbs obtained by end of life tires can be found in
[14]  while  data  on  the  sound  absorption  properties  of  loose  expanded  clay  are  reported  in  [15].
However  the  authors  are  aware  of  very  few  scientific  papers  reporting  experimental  data  of  the
sound  absorption  properties  of  stone  aggregates  [16],  while  no  data  concerning  transmission  loss
have been found in the literature. This latter aspect can be of interest if the material is used for cool
roofs applications, since it can bring to an increase in the sound insulation of the roof.

The acoustic characterization is performed by combining the findings of thermal-energy previous
studies with the results of acoustic in-lab measurement campaign carried out in this paper. In partic-
ular, several thicknesses of the gravel layer and grain sizes are tested in order to be able to compare

2.  Materials and methods

2.1  Characteristics of the stone

The investigated materials are low-cost stones from central Italy made almost at 100% of calci-
um  carbonate,  typically  used  as  aggregate  for  concrete  based  materials.  The  use  of  such  stone  as
roof covering is an original aspect of this research. Given the natural high thermal emissivity of all
the construction materials with no metal content and the stone light color, a reasonable good natural
behavior was assumed and studied in previous works by the same authors [10]. Four already com-
mercially available stone sizes has been chosen for the present analysis in order to integrate the op-
tic-thermal analysis carried out in [10] with the acoustic characterization which is the main object of
this  work.  In  particular,  Table  1  and  Fig.  1  report  grain  sizes  and  pictures  of  the  stones  used  for
building roof application. The grain sizes 3 and 4 are the most common sizes usually applied over
roof or external paved areas. Specific grain sizes were shown to affect the aggregate albedo which
increases from about 30% to about 50% with decreasing of grain size [10], as in-field measured by
the  authors.  The  selected  stone  type  was  chosen  also  for  being  a  local  sustainable  solution,  with
natural cool properties, since the albedo of generic gravels tested by the authors or other researchers
never exceeds 30% [9]. Being a local material, it also has the advantage of a reduced embodied en-
ergy, due to the low energy consumption for transportation.

2.2  Description of the methodology

Normal incidence absorption coefficient and transmission loss of the investigated samples were
measured using an impedance tube Brüel & Kjӕr, model 4260, connected to a signal analyser Brüel
& Kjӕr Pulse.

2.2.1  Normal incidence absorption coefficient

The  absorption  coefficient  was  measured  according  to  the  procedures  defined  by  the  standard
ISO 10534-2 [17]. The tube was mounted in vertical position and the stones were poured into the
sample holder trying to obtain an upper surface of the sample as much flat as possible (Fig. 2).

Two configurations of the tube were used: (i) the large tube, with a diameter of 100 mm in order
to investigate the frequency range of 50-1600 Hz and (ii) the small tube, with a diameter of 29 mm
to cover the frequency range of 500-6400 Hz. Then the results obtained by the two tubes for a sam-
ple were combined in order to obtain a single trend in the frequency range 50-6400 Hz.

The first available size, code 1_xl, was discarded since the voids created by the grains were too
big to achieve an adequate energy loss; moreover the size of the single grain was of the same order
of magnitude with respect to the small impedance tube. The remaining sizes, namely 2_l, 3_m and
4_s, were tested with four different values of thickness: 10 mm, 20 mm, 40 mm and 80 mm. The
first three values of thickness can be used when the stones are placed on a roof to improve the solar
reflectance or, more commonly, to protect the watertight sheath from the sun, wind, rain and other
meteorological causes of degradation. The 80 mm thickness was investigated for characterizing the
stones laid in external paved areas.

Each configuration was tested three times and the results averaged in order to  make the results

less sensitive to the heterogeneity of the studied material.

2.2.2  Transmission loss

The transmission loss was measured by means of the ‘two-load’ transfer  function method [18-
19], which is an extension of the transfer function method used for the normal incidence sound ab-
sorption measurements [17]. This method requires a four microphone impedance tube and four sets
of measurements: two without the sample under test with a rigid and an anechoic termination and
two in the same configurations with the sample under test. This method allows to evaluate the com-
plex amplitudes of the direct, reflected and propagated plane wave components in the upstream and

3.  Results

3.1.1  Normal incidence absorption coefficient

Fig. 4-6 report the trends of the normal incidence sound absorption coefficient in the frequency
range 100-6400 Hz for the three investigated grain sizes (2_l, 3_m and 4_s) and for four different
values of thickness (10 mm, 20 mm, 40 mm and 80 mm).

As expected, for the same grain size increasing values of the thickness lead to higher values of
the sound absorption coefficient and to a shift of the first peak of sound absorption towards lower
frequencies, as demonstrated in Fig. 4 for size 2_l and in Fig. 5 for size 3_m. The first absorption
peaks for 10 mm thick samples fall outside the investigated frequency range.  Furthermore for the
same thickness  the sound absorption  was found  to  be higher for smaller  grain  sizes,  according to
previous researches by the authors [14-15].

However these statements were not verified for the grain size 4_s: Fig. 6 shows that in this case
the expected trends are not obtained and the absorption coefficient keeps at a quite constant and low
value even changing the values of the thickness. This is mainly due to the fact that the smallest frac-
tion of the 4_s grain size filled the open pores created by the largest fraction and obstructed the air
flow inside the voids (see Fig. 2). Therefore the absorption mechanisms were due to the top layers
of  the  sample  only  and  small  changes  of  sound  absorption  were  detected  when  the  thickness  in-
creased. As far as the sound absorption, the best performing grain size is the one named 3_m.

3.1.2  Transmission loss

Fig. 7 reports the trends of the transmission loss in the frequency range 100-1600 Hz for the two
investigated  grain  sizes  (2_l  and  3_m)  for  different  values  of  thickness  (20  mm,  40  mm  and  80

The results show that for each investigated thickness the measured transmission loss of the grain
size 3_m is always 1-2 dB higher than the one measured for the grain size 4_l. As expected, trans-
mission loss increases with increasing values of the thickness, reaching a maximum value of 7 dB at
700 Hz for a 80 mm sample made of stones with grain size 3_m. The influence of the mesh used for
containing the stones inside the sample holder can be noticed around 300 Hz.

4.  Conclusions

The paper reports the results of an experimental campaign carried out at the Acoustics Lab of the
University of Perugia aimed at evaluating the sound absorption and the transmission loss of various
samples of local natural stones used by the authors [10] for cool roofs applications. Among the test-
ed grain sizes, the one showing the best acoustic performance, both for absorption and insulation, is
the one made of grains with diameters ranging from 2 mm to 5.6 mm: a 40 mm layer of these stones
showed to  achieve  relatively  good values of sound absorption  (0.5-0.9) in the frequency range of
1000-1500 Hz. In the same range the transmission loss was about 5-6 dB. This frequency range is
important  since  the  normalized  road  traffic  spectrum  defined  by  the  standard  EN  1793-3  [20]  is
centered at 1000 Hz. Thicker layers would bring to higher values of sound absorption and transmis-
sion loss but the weight would limit their use for cool roofs, whereas they could be considered for
installation on grounds, such as external paved areas.

Taking into account the results achieved by the authors in previous studies focused on the pas-
sive  cooling  potential  of  the  selected  natural  aggregates,  this  work  was  built  upon  the  thermal-
energy  and  environmental  performance  of  the  proposed  solution:  the  results  show  that  the  cool
gravel  could  be suitable  also  for acoustic purpose.  In particular the acoustic benefits  produced by
the stone aggregates are very well documented for the relatively fine grain size (3_m), according to
the albedo field measurements of the gravels, showing a relatively good solar reflectance values. In
fact, the small size of stones was characterized by about 45% value of albedo, i.e. more than 30%
more than a classic bitumen based membrane for roof application.

Finally, this research confirmed the promising potential of the sustainable, low cost, and local so-

lution as roof covering and urban paving from a both acoustic and thermal-energy point of view.
